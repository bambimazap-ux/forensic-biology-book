### Figure 11.1 — Friction-ridge skin morphogenesis, pattern topologies, histology, and permanence versus persistence

- **File**: `Fig11-01_morphogenesis-anatomy-patterns` (`.png` / `.svg`)
- **Placement**: Section 11.1, immediately following the sentence:
  > *"...The two are not interchangeable: broad ridge flow can remain recognizable while local ridge paths vary in appearance, and pores and ridge-edge shapes are less stable across observations and recording conditions [Mon19]."*
- **Caption**:
  **Figure 11.1: Friction-ridge skin morphogenesis, pattern topologies, histology, and permanence versus persistence.**
  *(A)* Prenatal friction-ridge morphogenesis: embryonic volar pads (temporary fetal swellings on digits and palms whose regression timing, geometry, and symmetry establish boundary stresses) [Mac09, Wer02, Glo23]; mechanical buckling model of epidermal compressive stresses (Kücken & Newell) [Kuc05] contrasted with epithelial reaction-diffusion signaling waves (Glover et al.: EDAR and WNT promote periodic ridge differentiation, BMP acts as a diffusible inhibitor; wave collision interfaces form triradii) [Glo23]; upstream limb-development genetic architecture [Li22].
  *(B)* Fundamental friction-ridge pattern topologies defined by triradii count: Arches (0 triradii; plain and tented arches lacking focal delta structures), Loops (1 triradius; radial and ulnar loops with core and single delta), and Whorls (2 or more triradii; plain whorls, central pocket loops, and double loops) [Li22, Glo23].
  *(C)* Histological cross-section of friction-ridge skin: stratified epidermis (stratum corneum down to basal layer) projecting primary ridges into the underlying vascularized dermis; dermal papillae interlocking with epidermal pegs; alternating furrows and secondary ridges; dermal eccrine sweat glands with secretory ducts traversing the epidermis to open as discrete pores along ridge apices; anatomical minutiae (ridge endings, bifurcations) arising from local primary ridge branching during morphogenesis [Wer02, Glo23].
  *(D)* Permanence versus persistence: biological permanence (lifelong morphological stability of friction-ridge skin in living tissue, governed by basal layer renewal) contrasted with impression persistence (variable reproducibility of transferred 2D marks across variable contact dynamics, deposition pressure, substrate texture, environmental exposure, and development reagents) [Mon19]. Anatomical minutiae and pores represent biological skin morphology, not source comparison features (reserved for Chapter 12). Simulated data markers indicated by `sim_tag()`.

### Figure 11.2 — Latent fingermark residue: chemical composition, secretory sources, and environmental aging

- **File**: `Fig11-02_residue-composition-sources-aging` (`.png` / `.svg`)
- **Placement**: Section 11.2, immediately following the sentence:
  > *"...Residue composition therefore guides the choice of development approach, but published profiles cannot be read as a prediction of the composition of a particular deposit [Bec26, Rob22]."*
- **Caption**:
  **Figure 11.2: Latent fingermark residue: chemical composition, secretory sources, and environmental aging.**
  *(A)* Dual biological glandular contributions: eccrine sweat glands (aqueous secretion comprising water, inorganic salts such as NaCl, and polar free amino acids including serine, glycine, alanine, leucine, and lysine) [Rob22] versus sebaceous glands (water-insoluble lipid-rich secretion transferred indirectly by touching the face, forehead, or hair: squalene, free fatty acids, triglycerides, wax esters, and cholesterol) [Rob22, Cro10].
  *(B)* Natural versus deliberately groomed deposits: empirical findings from Croxton et al. (2010), demonstrating that touching sebum-rich facial skin immediately prior to deposition selectively spikes squalene and fatty-acid content by over six-fold while leaving polar amino-acid abundance essentially unaltered [Cro10].
  *(C)* Exogenous contaminants and donor variability: contributions from cosmetics, personal care lotions, pharmaceuticals, occupational chemicals, food residues, and donor physiological status (age, sex, diet, stress) [Bec26, Rob22].
  *(D)* Post-deposition environmental alteration and degradation pathways: substrate interaction dynamics (capillary migration and cellulose binding in porous substrates versus fragile interfacial spreading on nonporous surfaces) and chemical breakdown pathways (squalene and unsaturated fatty-acid auto-oxidation, UV photodegradation, volatile loss, and microbial breakdown) [Bec26, Rob22].
  *(E)* Foundational trace principle: extreme inter-donor and intra-donor variability precludes any canonical chemical fingermark profile; published compositional data inform the selection of physicochemical development reagents but cannot predict the specific makeup of an individual casework mark [Rob22, Bec26]. ### Figure 11.3 — Fingermark detection on porous substrates: amino-acid reagents and physical developer

- **File**: `Fig11-03_porous-reagents-physical-developer` (`.png` / `.svg`)
- **Placement**: Section 11.3, immediately following the sentence:
  > *"...Such sequences are evidence to weigh against the specific substrate, inks, condition, and prior treatment—not a universal recipe."*
- **Caption**:
  **Figure 11.3: Fingermark detection on porous substrates: amino-acid reagents and physical developer.**
  *(A)* Porous substrate dynamics: capillary absorption of fingermark fluids into the three-dimensional cellulose fiber network; water-soluble components migrate into the bulk while amino acids bind firmly to cellulose fibers, exhibiting minimal lateral diffusion under ambient dry casework conditions [Jel09b, Bec20].
  *(B)* Amino-acid reagent chemical mechanisms and optical properties: (1) Ninhydrin Strecker-type oxidative decarboxylation reacting with $\alpha$-amino acids to yield Ruhemann's purple (visible absorption $\sim 570\text{ nm}$; non-fluorescent unless post-treated with zinc salts) [Jel09b]; (2) DFO (1,8-diazafluoren-9-one) imine reaction yielding a pale red-orange product with intense photoluminescence under green light ($\sim 530\text{ nm}$) [Jel09b]; and (3) 1,2-Indanedione (IND / IND-Zn) reacting with amino acids to form a pink fluorophore with intense luminescence boosted by zinc chloride complexation, offering superior optical contrast on patterned and coloured papers [Jel09b, Bec22].
  *(C)* Physical Developer (PD) silver redox chemistry: aqueous iron(II)/iron(III) redox couple reducing silver ions ($Ag^+$) to metallic silver nanoparticles ($Ag^0$); surfactant micelles maintain colloidal stability while metallic silver deposits selectively onto water-insoluble lipids and fatty acids; effectively rescues wet, water-soaked, or aged documents where amino acids have leached away [Sod16, Bec20].
  *(D)* Sequential processing on paper (IND-Zn $\rightarrow$ Ninhydrin $\rightarrow$ Physical Developer) and casework constraints: solvent formulation risks (excess methanol inducing ink bleeding), thermal paper coating darkening under organic solvents, and acid pre-wash requirements to neutralise alkaline paper fillers ($CaCO_3$) prior to PD [Mar14f, Bec20, Bec22]. ### Figure 11.4 — Nonporous, wet, and challenging surfaces: powders, cyanoacrylate fuming, fVMD, and SPR

- **File**: `Fig11-04_nonporous-cyanoacrylate-vmd-spr` (`.png` / `.svg`)
- **Placement**: Section 11.4, immediately following the sentence:
  > *"...They are research tools, not routine replacements for established powder, CA, or other substrate-directed processes [Bec26, Bec20, Bec22, Ble20]."*
- **Caption**:
  **Figure 11.4: Nonporous, wet, and challenging surfaces: powders, cyanoacrylate fuming, fVMD, and SPR.**
  *(A)* Nonporous surface interface and fingerprint powder adhesion: physical adherence of fine powder particles (flake powders: aluminium, carbon black) to surface-bound oily and moist residue on impermeable substrates (glass, plastics, metals); magnetic flake applicators reducing friction marks; health-and-safety cautions regarding inhalation hazards of dry nanoparticle formulations lacking proven operational casework gain [Bec26, Bec20].
  *(B)* Cyanoacrylate (superglue) fuming and fluorescent dye staining: heated volatilization of ethyl cyanoacrylate monomer in controlled 75% relative humidity; anionic chain polymerization initiated by carboxylate groups (lactate, amino acid carboxylates) along ridge deposits, forming a resilient white polycyanoacrylate polymer; secondary optical enhancement via fluorescent dye stains (Basic Yellow 40, Rhodamine 6G) excited under forensic light sources (FLS) with barrier filters [War07, Bec20, Bec22].
  *(C)* Forensic Vacuum Metal Deposition (fVMD): two-stage thermal evaporation under high vacuum ($10^{-4}\text{ mbar}$); minute gold evaporation provides nucleation sites across the surface, followed by selective zinc condensation onto gold-seeded regions; zinc avoids lipid-rich ridges, creating high-contrast negative marks on difficult substrates including polythene bags, fabrics, and polymer banknotes [Dov25].
  *(D)* Small Particle Reagent (SPR) and casework reality: aqueous surfactant suspension of fine molybdenum disulfide ($MoS_2$) particles adhering mechanically to water-insoluble lipids on wet outdoor items, rain-exposed vehicles, and adhesive tape [Bum16]; operational reality emphasizing that established physical and chemical processes remain the casework foundation, whereas advanced analytical chemical imaging (mass spectrometry, synchrotron infrared) remains an exploratory research capability [Bec26, Ble20]. ### Figure 11.5 — Process selection, sequential processing, and research validation methodology

- **File**: `Fig11-05_process-selection-research-methodology` (`.png` / `.svg`)
- **Placement**: Section 11.5, immediately following the sentence:
  > *"...Realistic trials, clearly reported item and process conditions, and comparisons that test each step’s added contribution provide a stronger basis for choosing a sequence than an isolated high-yield demonstration [Bec26, Mar14f, Sea12]."*
- **Caption**:
  **Figure 11.5: Process selection, sequential processing, and research validation methodology.**
  *(A)* The Sears Triad: fundamental forensic interaction model among Mark characteristics (natural vs groomed, eccrine vs sebaceous load, age, donor variability), Surface properties (porosity, texture, curvature, colour, cleanliness), and Environmental exposure (weather, rain, sunlight, temperature extremes) governing reagent suitability and sequence design [Sea12].
  *(B)* Preliminary non-destructive optical examination: systematic multi-spectral inspection under white light (oblique/diffuse), UV-A ($365\text{ nm}$ inherent fluorescence), and tunable forensic light sources (FLS; $450\text{ nm}$ blue with yellow filter, $530\text{ nm}$ green with orange filter, and infrared to penetrate dark ink patterns); non-destructive photographic documentation recovering ridge detail prior to chemical alteration [Bec26, Bec22].
  *(C)* Sequential processing decision workflows and downstream evidence preservation: porous sequencing (Optical $\rightarrow$ IND-Zn $\rightarrow$ Ninhydrin $\rightarrow$ Physical Developer) targeting amino acids before lipids [Mar14f]; nonporous sequencing (Optical $\rightarrow$ Powders/CA Fuming $\rightarrow$ Fluorescent Dyes $\rightarrow$ fVMD) [Bec20]; downstream evidence constraints: prioritizing touch DNA sampling or protecting swab areas from solvent immersion, preventing solvent-induced running of document inks, and safeguarding thermal coatings [Bec26, Bec22].
  *(D)* Sears three-phase research validation hierarchy: Phase 1 (laboratory screening on model marks and pristine substrates demonstrating chemical feasibility) $\rightarrow$ Phase 2 (pseudo-operational trials with realistic handled items and representative donors evaluating environmental decay and incremental step contribution) $\rightarrow$ Phase 3 (operational casework trials in accredited forensic laboratories); laboratory yield alone does not establish operational casework performance [Sea12, Bec26].
  *(E)* Cardinal development and recovery principle: physicochemical development techniques recover and enhance physical ridge detail on evidence substrates; they do not provide source comparison, individualization, or identification decisions (which are governed strictly by the ACE-V methodology and probabilistic models in Chapter 12) [Mon19, Bec26].
