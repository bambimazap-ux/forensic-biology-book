# Chapter 0 – figure captions and anchors

## Figure 0.1 — Fig00-01_hierarchy-propositions
Anchor: Section 0.3, after "…while offence-level propositions concern whether the person committed the alleged crime [Coo98, Gil20]."
Caption: **Figure 0.1. The hierarchy of propositions in forensic interpretation.** The four levels of propositions (sub-source, source, activity, offence), showing the question evaluated at each level and competing propositions ($H_p$ vs. $H_d$). A forensic expert evaluates sub-source, source, or activity levels depending on available case information and expertise, whereas the offence level is generally a matter for the court. Moving up the hierarchy requires additional contextual conditioning and cannot be inferred from a high sub-source likelihood ratio alone [Gil18, Gil20]. Source: Original illustration based on [Coo98, Gil18, Gil20, Eve15].
Interactive candidate: yes (clickable levels displaying generic propositions and conditioning requirements).

## Figure 0.2 — Fig00-02_likelihood-ratio-logic
Anchor: Section 0.3, after "…also called the transposed-conditional error [Eve15]."
Caption: **Figure 0.2. The logic of the likelihood ratio and the transposed-conditional fallacy.** (A) The likelihood ratio evaluates the probability of the forensic evidence $E$ under two competing propositions, $H_p$ and $H_d$. The expert reports this relative support, where $\mathrm{LR} = 1$ indicates that findings do not favour either proposition; the LR does not determine which proposition is true. (B) The prosecutor's fallacy transposes the condition, confusing $\Pr(E \mid H)$ (the probability of observing the evidence given a proposition) with $\Pr(H \mid E)$ (the probability that the proposition is true given the evidence) [Eve15]. Source: Original illustration based on [Eve15, Sak08, Col09, Gil18].
Interactive candidate: yes (interactive comparison of conditional probabilities illustrating how changing hypotheses affects $\Pr(E \mid H)$ versus $\Pr(H \mid E)$).

## Figure 0.3 — Fig00-03_linear-sequential-unmasking
Anchor: Section 0.5, after "…its objectivity, and its relevance to the decision [Dro21]."
Caption: **Figure 0.3. Information flow under Linear Sequential Unmasking (LSU and LSU-E).** (A) Standard LSU workflow in comparative examinations: an examiner documents an initial assessment of trace material before seeing a suspect reference, receives additional information as needed, and records changes in interpretation [Dro15]. (B) LSU-E (expanded) sequences case information according to potential to bias, objectivity, and relevance to the decision [Dro21]. These procedures make information flow deliberate and transparent without withholding essential information. Source: Original illustration based on [Dro15, Dro21, Coo19].
Interactive candidate: yes (interactive sequential unmasking module tracking documented trace assessments before and after unmasking reference data).

## Figure 0.4 — Fig00-04_dna-tech-evolution
Anchor: Section 0.6, after "…it does not remove the need to assess what a result means in the case at hand [But22]."
Caption: **Figure 0.4. Technological evolution of forensic biological identification.** Four successive eras of biological typing, progressing from protein markers through fragment length to sequence information: (1) Serological protein markers (blood groups and other protein polymorphisms; limited discrimination, e.g. in close family relationships [Job13]); (2) RFLP/VNTR profiling (multi-band length patterns; depended on relatively intact DNA in sufficient quantity, with demanding processing [Roe13]); (3) PCR-STR multiplexes (shorter DNA segments in standardised profiles; sensitive and multiplexed for routine comparison and database use, but length-based typing does not show sequence differences within same-length alleles [But15, Bru18, Had21]); and (4) Massively parallel sequencing (MPS; adds sequence information, distinguishes same-length alleles, and combines multiple marker types, requiring validated workflows, bioinformatics, and consistent nomenclature [Bru18, But22]). Source: Original illustration based on [Job13, Roe13, But15, Had21, Bru18, But22].
Interactive candidate: yes (interactive comparison across eras illustrating the type of data produced: protein agglutination, autoradiograph multi-band length patterns, STR electropherograms, and MPS sequence reads).
