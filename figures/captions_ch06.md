### Figure 6.1

- **File**: `Fig06-01_msy-biology-inheritance.png` / `Fig06-01_msy-biology-inheritance.svg`
- **Script**: `Fig06-01_msy-biology-inheritance.py`
- **Width**: Full text width (`FULL_W`, 16.0 cm)
- **Placement anchor**: Section 6.1, immediately following:
  > "...The MSY loci are not ordinarily reshuffled independently through X–Y recombination [Kay17c, Cou21]."
- **Caption**:
  > **Figure 6.1. MSY biology, paternal-lineage inheritance, and IBD versus IBS sharing.** (A) Chromosomal structure of the human Y chromosome: the pseudoautosomal regions (PAR1 and PAR2) pair and recombine with corresponding regions of the X chromosome during male meiosis, while the extensive intervening male-specific region of the Y (MSY) lacks routine X–Y recombination and is effectively haploid [Cou21]. (B) Paternal-lineage inheritance: because MSY loci are physically linked, they are transmitted together from father to sons as an intact haplotype across generations [Kay17c, Cou21]. (C) Identical by descent (IBD) versus identical by state (IBS): paternal relatives share haplotypes through common ancestry (IBD); unrelated men may carry the same observed haplotype by chance (IBS) [Kni22]. Occasional step-wise mutations introduce allelic differences between descendants over time while other loci preserve shared lineage evidence [Kay17c, Cou21]. (D) Fundamental forensic principle: a Y-STR match corresponds to a paternal lineage, not a unique individual [Kay17c, Cou21]. Source: Original illustration based on [Cou21, Kay17c, Kni22].

### Figure 6.2

- **File**: `Fig06-02_marker-systems-rm-ystrs.png` / `Fig06-02_marker-systems-rm-ystrs.svg`
- **Script**: `Fig06-02_marker-systems-rm-ystrs.py`
- **Width**: Full text width (`FULL_W`, 16.0 cm)
- **Placement anchor**: Section 6.2, immediately following:
  > "...if RM markers are used, thresholds should account for their mutation rates [Kay17c, Bal14]."
- **Caption**:
  > **Figure 6.2. Marker systems: conventional Y-STRs, phylogenetic SNP haplogroups, and rapidly mutating Y-STRs (RM Y-STRs).** (A) Y-SNP haplogroups: stable phylogenetic branches defined by shared derived single nucleotide polymorphisms trace paternal lineages and biogeographic history, but do not describe genome-wide ancestry or identify individuals [Cou21, Kni22]. (B) Conventional forensic Y-STRs (e.g. Yfiler, PowerPlex Y23, Yfiler Plus): multiplex panels with moderate mutation rates (~1 in 1,000) differentiate unrelated male lines but remain shared identically across close patrilineal relatives [Cou21, Kni22]. (C) Rapidly mutating Y-STRs (RM Y-STRs): panels with high mutation rates (>1 in 100) separate close paternal relatives (e.g. father–son pairs, brothers) whose conventional profiles match [Ral22]. (D) Interpretive trade-offs: higher mutation rates distinguish close relatives, but an observed mismatch cannot automatically exclude shared paternal kinship [Bal14]. RM markers are generally unsuitable for standard kinship testing unless mutation-corrected thresholds are applied [Kay17c, Bal14]. Even a full match across an expanded RM panel does not identify a unique individual [Ral21]. Source: Original illustration based on [Cou21, Kni22, Bal14, Ral21, Ral22, Kay17c].

### Figure 6.3

- **File**: `Fig06-03_female-excess-profiling.png` / `Fig06-03_female-excess-profiling.svg`
- **Script**: `Fig06-03_female-excess-profiling.py`
- **Width**: Full text width (`FULL_W`, 16.0 cm)
- **Placement anchor**: Section 6.3, immediately following:
  > "...Y-STRs therefore complement, rather than replace, autosomal testing."
- **Caption**:
  > **Figure 6.3. Male-specific profiling in sexual-assault and female-excess samples.** (A) The female cellular excess challenge: in sexual-assault evidence (e.g. vaginal swabs, fingernail scrapings, or non-ejaculatory contact), minor male DNA is frequently masked by an overwhelming excess of female victim cellular material [Pur15, Cos25e]. (B) Autosomal versus Y-STR amplification contrast: under extreme female excess (e.g. 1,000:1 ratio), autosomal profiling leaves minor male alleles masked by female peaks, high stutter, or falling below analytical detection thresholds (<100 RFU). In contrast, male-specific Y-STR primers target MSY loci exclusively with zero cross-reactivity or background signal from female DNA, recovering clean, unambiguous male profiles [Pur15]. (C) Evidentiary and activity-level caveats: a matching Y-STR profile represents non-exclusion of a paternal lineage, not individual identification [Cou21]; detection does not establish contact type, deposition mechanism, or timing [Cos25e]; background male DNA is documented on female garments [Cos25e]; and separate testing consumes limited evidence extract [Cou21]. Simulated data for illustration. Source: Original illustration based on [Pur15, Cou21, Cos25e, Ral21].

### Figure 6.4

- **File**: `Fig06-04_yhrd-frequency-estimation.png` / `Fig06-04_yhrd-frequency-estimation.svg`
- **Script**: `Fig06-04_yhrd-frequency-estimation.py`
- **Width**: Full text width (`FULL_W`, 16.0 cm)
- **Placement anchor**: Section 6.4, immediately following:
  > "...No estimator removes the need to assess marker compatibility, population relevance, sample composition, and model assumptions before reporting a frequency [Roe20]."
- **Caption**:
  > **Figure 6.4. Haplotype reference databases and frequency estimation methods.** (A) YHRD reference framework: the Y-Chromosome Haplotype Reference Database (YHRD) indexes submitted population data by multiplex kit and ancestry metapopulation [Wil15b, Roe20]. Database counts reflect sample recurrence rather than fixed population frequencies, and depend on sample size $N$ and marker coverage. (B) Frequency estimation methods: (1) counting ($x/N$) and augmented counting ($(x+1)/(N+1)$) link directly to observed data, but zero observations ($x=0$) do not indicate absence from the population, and rare counts remain sensitive to sample size [Roe20, And13]; (2) the discrete Laplace method models repeat-step differences around inferred central founder haplotypes to estimate non-zero frequencies for both observed and unobserved profiles, assuming integer repeat steps [And13]; (3) graphical models (Chow–Liu trees and t-cherry extensions) represent inter-locus linkage dependencies across the MSY [And18b, And19]. (C) Mandatory casework verification: no estimator eliminates the duty to assess marker compatibility, population relevance, and model assumptions prior to reporting [Roe20]. Source: Original illustration based on [Wil15b, Roe20, And13, And18b, And19].

### Figure 6.5

- **File**: `Fig06-05_isfg-reporting-framework.png` / `Fig06-05_isfg-reporting-framework.svg`
- **Script**: `Fig06-05_isfg-reporting-framework.py`
- **Width**: Full text width (`FULL_W`, 16.0 cm)
- **Placement anchor**: Section 6.7, immediately following:
  > "...A source-level correspondence does not establish when or how DNA was deposited, so reporting should keep the supported comparison distinct from any activity-level conclusion [Roe20, Cou21]."
- **Caption**:
  > **Figure 6.5. ISFG decision pathways, lineage propositions, and reporting framework.** (A) Decision-based reporting pathway: evaluation proceeds sequentially from the case question (source, kinship, intelligence) to categorical triage (inconclusive, exclusion, non-exclusion) [Roe20, Cou21]. A non-exclusion warrants quantitative evaluation (likelihood ratio) only when suitable reference population data and estimation methods exist; otherwise, a qualified qualitative assessment is reported [Roe20]. (B) Lineage propositions and plausible alternatives: evaluating a Y-STR match requires defining alternative sources representing plausible paternal relatives rather than solely an abstract population of unrelated men [And21b, Bri24]. Rarity in a reference database does not establish individualization, as hundreds of patrilineal relatives in the wider population may share the profile [And17b, And21b]. (C) Mandatory reporting safeguards: reports must state marker inventory, profile completeness, database release and accession date; explain the likelihood ratio as evidence probability under competing propositions without transposing the conditional; and maintain strict separation between source-level correspondence and activity-level deposition claims [Roe20, Reb20, Cou21]. Source: Original illustration based on [Roe20, Reb20, Cou21, And21b, Bri24, Wil15b, And17b].
